One Pattern for Colors & Physical Traits

Arabic colors — and certain physical descriptions like “blind” or “deaf” — all follow one special three-part pattern. It even connects back to two rules you've already learned: diptotes, and the feminine ending اء.

أَفْعَلُ · فَعْلَاءُ · فُعْلٌ

From the Qur'an

The exact same forms, in revelation

وَمِنَ الْجِبَالِ جُدَدٌ بِيضٌ وَحُمْرٌ مُّخْتَلِفٌ أَلْوَانُهَا وَغَرَابِيبُ سُودٌ
“And among the mountains are tracts, white and red of varying shades, and [some] extremely black” — Sūrah Fāṭir, 35:27. All three plurals appear together.
...صَفْرَاءُ فَاقِعٌ لَّوْنُهَا تَسُرُّ النَّاظِرِينَ
“a yellow cow, bright in color, pleasing to the observer” — Sūrah al-Baqarah, 2:69. بَقَرَة (cow) is feminine, matched by صَفْرَاءُ.
صُمٌّ بُكْمٌ عُمْيٌ فَهُمْ لَا يَرْجِعُونَ
“Deaf, dumb, and blind — so they will not return [to the right path]” — Sūrah al-Baqarah, 2:18. Three plural forms, back to back.
وَمَن كَانَ فِي هَٰذِهِ أَعْمَىٰ فَهُوَ فِي الْآخِرَةِ أَعْمَىٰ
“And whoever is blind in this [life] will be blind in the Hereafter” — Sūrah al-Isrā’, 17:72.
Connecting the dots

Why these singular forms are diptote

Both أَحْمَرُ and حَمْرَاءُ refuse tanwīn — for two different reasons you've already met.

Masculine — أَحْمَرُ
أَحْمَرُ

Matches a special adjective pattern the same way a name can match a verb's shape.

— echoes the Diptotes lesson
Feminine — حَمْرَاءُ
حَمْرَاءُ

Ends in ـاء (alif mamdūda) — one of the three feminine signs from your very first lesson.

— echoes Word Types & Feminine Signs
i
Both forms drop the tanwīn and take a fatḥa in the genitive, exactly like any other diptote.
One exception

But the plural behaves normally!

Even though the singular forms are diptote, the plural فُعْلٌ is an ordinary broken plural — it takes tanwīn just fine.

Singular — diptote
أَحْمَرُ

no tanwīn

Plural — ordinary
حُمْرٌ

takes tanwīn normally

✓
You saw this already in Fāṭir 35:27 — بِيضٌ, حُمْرٌ, and سُودٌ all carry tanwīn, unlike their singular counterparts أَبْيَضُ, أَحْمَرُ, أَسْوَدُ.
Recap

The whole pattern, in one table

Masculineأَفْعَلُ pattern — e.g. أَحْمَرُ — diptote, matches a special adjective shape.
Feminineفَعْلَاءُ pattern — e.g. حَمْرَاءُ — diptote, ends in the alif mamdūda feminine sign.
Pluralفُعْلٌ pattern — e.g. حُمْرٌ — an ordinary broken plural, takes tanwīn normally.
